b) Because the library is closed on Sundays, students therefore study in cafés.

## Part B: Subjective vs. objective language (20 points)

**4.** Label each statement **O** (opinion), **F-NP** (fact that needs proof) or **SSD** (specific supporting detail). The figures are invented for this exercise. (6 × 1 = 6 points)

a) University students spend too much time on social media.
b) Many students use social media during study time.
c) In a 2024 survey of 1,200 students at one university, 64 percent reported checking social media at least once every hour while studying.
d) Social media is the worst distraction a student can have.
e) Students' sleep patterns are affected by late-night phone use.
f) "Students who kept their phones in another room scored on average 11 points higher on the test," the study's lead researcher reported.

**5.** Rewrite each sentence in objective academic style. Use a different technique each time and name it (passive voice, hedging, avoiding personal pronoun, moving information, replacing a phrasal verb, removing emotive language). (6 × 2 = 12 points)

b) The researchers found out that sleep deprivation has a huge, terrible effect on memory.
c) We interviewed 40 teachers in March 2025.
d) Smith's (2020) amazing study proves that homework is useless.
e) Video games will make teenagers violent.

**6.** Name **two** words or features in the sentence below that make it subjective. (2 points)

## Part C: Paragraph (25 points)

**7.** Decide whether each sentence is a simple fact (**F**), too broad for one paragraph (**B**), or a good topic sentence (**TS**). (5 × 1 = 5 points)

a) The cafeteria opens at 7:30 a.m.
b) Technology has changed human history.
c) Living with a roommate requires a great deal of patience.
d) Our professor gave us three assignments last week.
e) Part-time jobs can help university students develop useful skills.

**8.** Find the topic sentence (**TS**), the support sentences (**S**) and the unrelated sentence (**U**). (3 points)

a) Many students choose to study in the library.
b) The library has comfortable chairs and large tables.
c) The library is quiet, so students can concentrate.
d) The university was founded in 2004.

**9.** The paragraph below has one sentence that breaks its **unity**. Write the sentence and explain why in one sentence. (3 points)

> Regular exercise brings several benefits for university students. Students who exercise regularly tend to sleep better, which helps them stay focused in class. Exercise is also a healthy way to reduce exam stress. Many universities in Europe were founded in the Middle Ages. In addition, team sports give students a chance to make new friends.

**10.** Fill in the gaps with suitable transition signals. Use each one once: *however, for example, as a result, in addition, because, such as.* (6 × 0.5 = 3 points)

> Studying abroad can be a valuable experience. (1) ______, students often improve their language skills (2) ______ they use a foreign language every day. (3) ______, they learn to live independently. Daily tasks (4) ______ cooking, paying bills and dealing with banks help them become more responsible. (5) ______, studying abroad is not easy for everyone. Some students feel homesick and, (6) ______, their grades may drop in the first semester.

**11.** Write a paragraph of **120-150 words** on the topic below. Include a clear topic sentence (topic + controlling idea), at least three supporting points, transition signals and a concluding sentence. Use objective language. (11 points)

> Topic: **The challenges of the first year at university**

Grading: topic sentence 2, support and completeness 3, unity and logical order 2, coherence (transitions) 2, objective language and accuracy 2.

**13.** Fill in: *Dear Sir/Madam* is followed by "Yours ______", and *Dear Ms Smith* is followed by "Yours ______". (1 point)

**14.** Write an email to your professor (**Professor Kovač**, ELIT100, Section 3). ## Part F: Plagiarism and APA (15 points)

**17.** Is each case plagiarism? Write **Yes** or **No** and give a short reason. (5 × 1 = 5 points)

a) You copy two sentences from a website, put them in quotation marks, but do not name the source.
b) You change a few words in a paragraph from a book, keep the same sentence structure, and add (Brown, 2018).
c) You write "Water boils at 100 °C at sea level" without a citation.
d) You submit the same essay you wrote for another course last semester.
e) You rewrite an author's idea completely in your own words and add (Kaya, 2022).

**18.** Correct the APA 7 in-text citations. Each has at least one mistake. (5 × 1 = 5 points)

a) (Johnson and Smith, 2019)
b) Brown & Davis (2021) argue that...
c) (Martin, Clark, Lewis, 2020) — first citation of a source with three authors
d) "Language shapes thought" (Wilson, 2018).  — direct quotation from page 12
e) (Unicef 2023)

**19.** Answer briefly. (5 × 1 = 5 points)

a) What does *et al.* stand for and mean?
b) How many words does a quotation need to be formatted as a block quotation in APA 7?
c) Give two rules for formatting a block quotation.
d) A website has no publication date. How do you show this in an in-text citation?
e) Is "Mozart was the most talented composer who ever lived" common knowledge? Why or why not?
